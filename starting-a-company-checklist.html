Starting a Company: A Simple Checklist Before You Register

  • Company Setup
  • 08.OCT.2026
  • 3 MIN READ

Registering a company is an exciting step, but it is also where many new business owners run into delays, extra costs or problems that only show up months later. Most of these issues can be avoided with a little preparation before you submit any paperwork.

Exact requirements depend on the country, your industry and the type of company you choose, so always confirm the details with the relevant authorities or an adviser. The checklist below covers the main things to think about first.

1. Be clear about what your business will do

Write a short description of your main products or services, who your customers are and where you will operate. This affects the type of company you can register, the licences you need and sometimes even who is allowed to own it. Being specific now saves changes later.

2. Choose the right business structure

Common options include sole proprietorships, partnerships and limited companies. Each has different rules about ownership, personal liability, tax and reporting. Think about:

  • How many owners or shareholders the business will have
  • Whether you want to limit your personal liability for business debts
  • Your plans to grow, raise investment or bring in partners later
  • The ongoing reporting and compliance each structure requires

3. Check ownership rules

Some industries have rules about who can own a business, especially for foreign investors. Check early whether any restrictions or special approvals apply to your activity, so you can plan the right ownership structure from the start.

4. Prepare your company details and documents

Before you register, you will usually need to decide on and prepare:

  • A company name, and a check that it is available
  • A registered business address
  • Details and identification documents for owners and directors
  • The share structure and how much capital will be invested
  • Company rules or articles, where required

5. Identify the licences and permits you need

Registration alone is often not enough to start trading. Many activities need additional licences or permits, for example in food, education, health, construction or financial services. Find out what applies to you, how long it takes and what documents are needed.

6. Plan for tax registration and compliance

Understand which taxes your business will need to register for and what regular filings are expected. Put the key deadlines in a calendar from day one. Missing filings in the first months is a common and avoidable cause of penalties.

7. Set up banking and bookkeeping early

Open a separate business bank account and keep business and personal money apart. Decide how you will record income and expenses, whether in a simple spreadsheet, accounting software or with a bookkeeper. Good records from the beginning make tax filings easier and give you a clear view of how the business is doing.

8. Prepare for your first hires

If you plan to employ staff, find out what is required for employment contracts, payroll and any social security or similar registrations. Clear contracts and job descriptions from the start help avoid misunderstandings later.

Preparation is cheaper than correction. An hour spent planning now can save weeks of fixing problems after registration.

Starting a company involves many steps, and the rules can be confusing, especially if you are entering a new market. Working with an experienced adviser can help you choose the right structure, prepare your documents correctly and get operating faster.
